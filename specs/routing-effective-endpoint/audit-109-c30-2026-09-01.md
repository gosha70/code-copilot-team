## Verdict

**#109 is 33/33 against the AMENDED contract.**

This is **not** a claim that the original requirement became met. The
original C30 endpoint component and the §11 clause "sanitized upstream
endpoint — not only a loopback proxy" were **superseded on #109 on
2026-09-01, before any implementation**, because they require
information outside CCT's observation boundary for opaque gateways.
The amendment is cited below, and FR-E6 requires this audit to say so
in exactly these terms.

Three things this audit changes relative to its predecessors:

1. **Criterion 14 is re-derived, not inherited** — and it needed its
   own explicit amendment, which this audit obtained before issuing a
   verdict. Its prior "met" rested on reasoning PR #276 had already
   withdrawn. The verdict survives; the reason does not.
2. **The contract text on #109 was corrected to match merged code.**
   The amendment's provenance vocabulary still enumerated the
   first-draft aliases, and `effective_upstream.origin` was still typed
   as always-`null`. Both are now the merged FR-E8 vocabulary and the
   corrected type. Details below.
3. **C30's amended component 5 is met with a stated limitation** —
   codex's configured origin is *unestablished*, not *absent*.

### Contract-text corrections made before this verdict

An audit cannot establish 33/33 against a contract whose text disagrees
with the implementation it is auditing. Two disagreements were found in
review and corrected **on #109 itself**, not merely noted here:

| On the issue, before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `explicit_config \| backend_config \| backend_default \| none` | `profile_base_url \| profile_base_url_env \| codex_model_provider \| backend_default \| none` | the first two were first-draft aliases, replaced during plan review by semantically specific values; the plan calls the vocabulary CLOSED and rejects synonyms, so the acceptance source cannot keep enumerating retired ones |

These are corrections of contract TEXT to match the reviewed and merged
contract — not a widening of what the amendment concedes.

## The amended criterion, as it now reads live on #109

> Tokens, costs, failed verifier commands, repair cycles, **configured
> execution origin with its provenance and an explicit
> effective-upstream verification state** (AMENDED 2026-09-01; the
> original read "effective endpoint"), and effective model are
> accurately recorded.

Conjunctive. Six components; component 5 has three parts.

| # | Component | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | tokens | met | executed |
| 2 | costs | met | executed |
| 3 | failed verifier commands | met | `cooldown-supervisor.sh:1279`, durable at `$RT_DIR/verifiers-<round>.txt` |
| 4 | repair cycles | met | `rounds` at `:1133`, `:1294`, `:1302` |
| 5 | configured origin + provenance + verification state | **met, with a stated limitation** | executed, all launch paths |
| 6 | effective model | met | tri-state; unverified is `null` and is never priced |

### 1 and 2 — tokens and costs

Composed from merged code, from a real backend usage stream:

```json
{"tokens":{"input":120,"output":30,"cache_read":50,"cache_write":0,"status":"reported"},
 "cost":{"usd":0.000825,"basis":"computed","price_version":"2026-05-01"}}
```

### 6 — effective model, and the rule that binds 2 to 6

An unverified effective model is `null`, and pricing refuses to attach
to a requested model:

```json
{"effective_model":null,"basis":"unavailable","usd":null}
```

A requested model never proves what served the request, so it never
prices one. Component 2 cannot be satisfied by inventing component 6.

### 5 — the amended component, executed across every launch path

Real `rt_launch_env` → `rr_result`, at merged HEAD:

| Launch path | `upstream_origin` | `upstream_origin_source` | `effective_upstream` |
|---|---|---|---|
| registry `base_url` literal | `https://literal-host:9000` | `profile_base_url` | unverifiable/null/none |
| resolved `base_url_env` | `https://env-host:8000` | `profile_base_url_env` | unverifiable/null/none |
| **gateway** (`localhost:8787`) | `https://localhost:8787` | `profile_base_url_env` | unverifiable/null/none |
| login mode | `null` | `none` | unverifiable/null/none |
| `base_url_env` unset | `null` | `none` | unverifiable/null/none |
| non-http(s) resolved value | `null` | `none` | unverifiable/null/none |
| **codex, with a RESOLVABLE user config present** | `null` | `none` | unverifiable/null/none |

Four properties this establishes, each of which the amendment demands:

- **The gateway row is the point.** The configured origin records the
  gateway *as the gateway*, and the verification state stays
  `unverifiable`. The amendment's "never infer the downstream provider
  from a loopback proxy, gateway, endpoint reference, or configured
  origin" holds by construction, not by convention: the state machine
  in `rr_upstream_invariant` refuses `verified` without
  `provider_reported` evidence, and no code path can produce it.
- **Credentials do not survive.** `https://user:s3cr3t@Env-Host:8000/v1?k=1`
  records as `https://env-host:8000` — credentials, path, query and
  fragment stripped, host case normalized.
- **Unestablished is `none`, never an assumption.** Login mode records
  `none`, not `backend_default`; a test greps that no path assigns
  `backend_default` at all.
- **Provenance follows the resolved origin, not the reference form.**
  An unset or unusable `base_url_env` records `none` — naming
  `profile_base_url_env` there would attribute a source to a fact that
  was never established.

#### The limitation, stated rather than buried

**Codex's configured execution origin is UNESTABLISHED, not absent.**
A codex user config containing a resolvable provider `base_url` is
present in the executed evidence above, and CCT still records
`null` / `none`.

That is deliberate, and it is the correct record. T4 implemented the
resolution and withdrew it: `${CODEX_HOME:-$HOME/.codex}/config.toml`
is one LAYER of codex's configuration, not the configuration. Verified
against the codex-cli 0.147.0 this repo targets, from the shipped CLI:

| Flag | Documented behaviour |
|---|---|
| `-p, --profile <name>` | "Layer `$CODEX_HOME/<name>.config.toml` on top of the base user config" |
| `--ignore-user-config` | "Do not load `$CODEX_HOME/config.toml`" |

A higher-precedence layer can redefine `model_providers`, so reading
that one layer could durably record `base_url` X while codex resolved
the same selected provider to Y.

**Why this still meets the criterion.** The criterion requires accurate
RECORDING, and `none` is a positive, defined claim — "no configured
execution origin was established" — not an omission. The record asserts
nothing false. What it does not do is *establish* codex's configured
origin, and a reader of a codex routed result must not read `null` as
"codex used no endpoint".

**Why the alternative would have failed the criterion.** Recording the
user-layer value would have been *inaccurate* recording whenever a
higher-precedence layer disagreed — the same class of defect as the
withdrawn `endpoint_ref` verdict, in new clothing.

`codex_model_provider` remains in the closed FR-E8 vocabulary, reserved
for a path that reproduces codex's own layered resolution. See #281 for
the separate, non-blocking defect in the benchmark backend's copy of
this problem.

## Criterion 14 — re-derived, and it required its own amendment

> ~~The actual upstream endpoint/context limit is recorded and
> enforced.~~ **AMENDED 2026-09-01.** Replaced by: *The configured
> execution origin with its provenance, and an explicit
> effective-upstream verification state, are recorded; the actual
> upstream context limit is recorded and enforced.*

This criterion contains **the same phrase whose C30 counterpart PR #276
withdrew**, and neither prior audit revisited it: the 2026-08-31 audit
recorded "met (increment F)", and
`specs/routing-context-limit/plan.md` justified the endpoint half as
"shipped with increment B" — which is `endpoint_ref` journaling, the
exact evidence PR #276 ruled is *not* recording the endpoint. The prior
verdict was correct in outcome and wrong in its stated reason.

### A correction to this audit's own first draft

The first draft of this audit resolved that by **inference from an
adjacent bullet**: criterion 14's two halves map to two neighbouring
§11 telemetry items, one of which the amendment had superseded, so the
draft treated criterion 14's endpoint half as superseded along with it.

**That was not sound, and it was refused in review.** The live
amendment named C30's "effective endpoint" and the §11 clause — and
nothing else. An audit does not get to widen an amendment's scope by
adjacency; doing so is the same move as reading a journaled reference
as a recorded endpoint, one level up. An amendment either names a
criterion or does not govern it.

### What was done instead

**Criterion 14 was amended explicitly on #109**, in its own words,
before this audit's verdict. The amendment section now names it
alongside C30 and the §11 clause, records why the first draft omitted
it, and preserves the original struck through. The criteria count is
unchanged at 33.

The split is stated in the replacement text rather than inferred:

- **endpoint half → superseded**, and met by the same amended contract
  and the same executed evidence as C30's component 5 above;
- **context-limit half → unchanged**, and met by increment F. That is
  the half "enforced" attaches to: `rt_select` takes a
  `min-context-tokens` argument (`routing-select.sh:65`) and filters on
  `min(declared, applicable observed)`, with no declaration meaning
  UNKNOWN and therefore ineligible. #109's own §Risks states the same
  intent — "mitigated by enforcing the actual upstream limit rather
  than the client-advertised limit."

**Verdict: met** — on an explicit amendment plus #277/#280, not on
`endpoint_ref` and not on adjacency.

## What this audit does NOT claim

- **Not that the original C30 became met.** It was superseded before
  implementation, on the owner's instruction, recorded on the issue
  itself. Any future reader who wants the original requirement wants a
  new observation seam, and the survey in
  `specs/routing-effective-endpoint/plan.md` explains why none exists.
- **Not that codex's endpoint is known.** It is recorded as
  unestablished. That is an accurate record, not a resolved one.
- **Not that any effective upstream is verified.** No code path in this
  tree can produce `effective_upstream.status: verified`; the field
  exists so that absence is stated rather than implied. Every routed
  result today is `unverifiable`/`null`/`none`.
- **No live routed run was executed.** The evidence is composed through
  the real `rt_launch_env` → `rr_result` path with real fixtures, at
  merged HEAD — not a live backend invocation. A live run could reveal
  a provider-reported signal no fixture contains, which is why option
  O3 remains "blocked on evidence" rather than rejected.
- **Nothing here closes #109 or #273.** This audit establishes the
  verdict; closing them is the owner's decision.
